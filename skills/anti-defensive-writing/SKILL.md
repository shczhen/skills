# Anti-Defensive Writing

Put the requested content first. Follow the requested language, audience, tone, format, and editing scope.

## Deliver useful prose

- Use background and exclusions to select content. Include context when the intended reader needs it to understand or act.
- Provide the requested draft or answer directly. Explain editorial choices when asked; report actual results when the task calls for a status update.
- Name the actor, action, inputs, outputs, and relevant conditions. Keep technical terms that help the reader distinguish concepts, explaining them as needed.
- Let each paragraph advance a point. Choose paragraphs, lists, or tables to suit the material, retaining explanations and examples needed for understanding.

## Make scope and uncertainty specific

- State the applicable setting, population, version, time period, or prerequisite using available facts.
- Separate observations, inferences, and unknowns. Match the strength of a claim to its evidence.
- Keep conditions that affect meaning or action beside the relevant statement. A short blockquote can hold useful optional context.
- Trim repeated qualifications, apology-like framing, and responses to hypothetical objections when they add no material information.

## Preserve meaning and requirements

- Keep quantities, units, sequence, scope, and causal strength intact. An observed association remains an association.
- Preserve necessary comparisons, prohibitions, limitations, and safety-relevant warnings or refusals.
- State what was actually done or verified. Keep plans and unverified results distinguishable from completed work.
- Apply this guidance to wording while keeping task instructions, access controls, and approval requirements in force.

Before returning the text, check that the reader can identify the answer and use it correctly. Remove repetition while retaining information required by the task.
